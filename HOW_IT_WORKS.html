IFP Subsidy Platform — how it works
A plain-language walkthrough for the people who will use it
Walkthrough

What this system does

A Chartered Accountant firm prepares Gujarat IFP subsidy applications for its clients. Today that means a spreadsheet per client, a folder of scanned documents, and one person re-typing the same forty fields into the government portal at eleven at night. This platform holds the case instead — and when the application is finally ready, types it into the portal form for you.

It does not file the application.

It never presses submit, never solves the CAPTCHA, never logs in to the portal for you, and never holds your portal password. The last click is always a human's. That is a deliberate design limit, not a feature that is coming later — see Limits & safety.

The three pieces

1 · Web app
The case file
Clients, cases, project and finance details, documents, consent, checks. This is where the work happens.
2 · Browser extension
The typing
Takes a finished case and fills the portal form, marking every box it touched. You review and submit.
3 · Super admin
The firms
Creates firms, sets their licence and limits, suspends them. Cannot see inside any of them.

Why a case, and not a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet will hold the same numbers. What it will not do is tell you that the commercial production date is before the loan sanction date, that the Udyam number does not match the entity type, or that you are missing the bank sanction letter — before the file reaches the portal, rather than six weeks later in a query memo.

Each case carries five things a spreadsheet does not:

WhatWhy it matters
Readiness scoreOne number saying how close this case is to being fileable, so you know which of your 40 cases to open this morning.
Automatic checks21 structural checks run on demand — formats, arithmetic, date ordering, required fields, documents, consent. Blocking problems stop the case from being marked ready.
Document checklistWhich papers the scheme requires for this application type, and which are still missing.
Client consentRecorded, timestamped and versioned, because you are handling someone's PAN and bank details on their behalf.
An audit trailWho opened what, who changed what, who revealed an identifier, and when. It cannot be edited by anyone, including a firm admin.

Where your data sits

Each firm is sealed off

Two independent walls separate one firm's data from another's: the application will not build a query without the firm attached, and the database itself refuses to return a row from another firm even if the application asks wrongly. Either one alone would be enough on a good day. Both together is what you want on a bad one.

Identifiers are encrypted, not merely hidden

PAN, GSTIN, Udyam and loan account numbers are stored encrypted with a key belonging to your firm alone, and they leave the system masked (ABCDE••••F). Seeing a full value takes a specific permission and writes an audit line naming the person who looked. Aadhaar is stored as the last four digits only.

One case, start to finish

This is the whole working day, in order. Nothing here is automatic except the checks — every step is something a person decides to do.

Client→ Case→ Details→ Checks→ Documents→ Consent→ Ready to file→ Portal→ You submit
  1. Staff or CA · once per client

    Add the client

    The business applying for the subsidy: its legal name, entity type, PAN, GSTIN, Udyam registration, address, and the promoters behind it. You enter this once; every future case for that client reuses it.

    If someone has already added that PAN, the system says so instead of creating a second copy. Two half-filled records for the same business is how a wrong figure ends up in a filing.

  2. CA

    Open a case

    A case is one application: this client, this scheme, this application type — capital subsidy, interest subsidy, provisional or final eligibility. It gets a case number and starts as a Draft.

    A client with three applications running has three cases. They do not share a status, a checklist, or a deadline, because they do not share a fate.

  3. Staff or CA

    Fill in the sections

    The case is divided the way the application itself is divided:

    SectionWhat goes in
    ProjectWhere the unit is, what it makes, land and building, plant and machinery, commercial production date.
    LoanBank, branch, sanction number and date, amount, rate, tenure, moratorium.
    InvestmentThe capital breakdown the subsidy is calculated on.
    EmploymentHeadcount, split by category as the scheme asks for it.
    First saleFirst sale invoice and date — the anchor for several eligibility windows.

    The readiness score moves as you go. You can stop at any point; nothing is lost and nothing is submitted.

  4. Anyone · as often as you like

    Run the checks

    Press Run validation and the case is examined against the scheme's structural rules. Each finding lands in one of three buckets:

    Blocking  Must be fixed. The case cannot be marked ready to file.

    Warning  Probably wrong. You can proceed, knowingly.

    Info  Worth a look. Nothing is blocked.

    Read this one carefully.

    The checks that run are structural — formats, arithmetic, dates in the right order, required fields present. A further set of rules about eligibility (turnover bands, subsidy percentages, qualifying periods) depends on the Government Resolution that constitutes the scheme, and that document has not been read by anyone building this. Those rules are switched off, and the screen lists exactly which ones. A clean validation does not mean the client is eligible. It means the paperwork is internally consistent.

  5. Staff or CA

    Work the document checklist

    The scheme's required papers for this application type appear as a list, each one marked Required, Present, Not available or Not applicable. Marking something "not available" is a decision the file records, so nobody has to remember in March why a certificate was left out in January.

  6. CA only

    Record the client's consent

    You are handling someone's PAN, bank details and business figures. Before the case can be marked ready, the client's consent is recorded against the exact version of the consent text they agreed to — so if the wording changes later, the file still shows what they actually saw.

  7. CA or firm admin

    Mark it ready to file

    The case moves Draft → In review → Ready to file. The system refuses the last step while a blocking check is unresolved or consent is missing. This is the one gate in the system that says no.

  8. CA · on the government portal

    File it — yourself

    Open ifp.gujarat.gov.in, sign in with your own credentials, and open the application form. Click the extension, pick the case, press Fill form. It types what it knows and highlights every box it touched.

    Then you check the form, solve the CAPTCHA, and press submit. Afterwards, come back and move the case to Filed with the acknowledgement number.

After filing

The case keeps moving: Filed → Acknowledged → possibly Query raised → Approved or Rejected. Every transition records who made it and when, so the history of a two-year application is one screen rather than an email thread.

The browser extension

A small Chrome add-on that takes a case you have already prepared and types it into the portal's application form. It is the only part of this system that touches the government website at all.

What it does

  • Types values into boxes on the form
  • Outlines every box it filled in green
  • Outlines what it skipped in amber, and says why
  • Warns you if the portal's form has changed since it was taught
  • Runs only when you click it, only on the portal

What it will never do

  • Press submit — there is no code that could
  • Read, solve or forward a CAPTCHA
  • Read a password box, or store your portal login
  • Run on its own, on a timer, or in the background
  • Send back any value it typed

Setting it up — once

  1. Install it

    In Chrome or Edge, open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, choose Load unpacked and select the apps/extension folder. The IFP icon appears in the toolbar. (Pin it — you will use it often.)

  2. Pair it with your firm

    In the web app, open Browser extension → Pair a browser. You get a short code, good for ten minutes and usable once. Click the extension, paste the code, and give the browser a name you will recognise later — "office desktop", "Rakesh laptop".

    Your portal password is never entered here.

    Pairing links the extension to your platform account, not to the government portal. The extension works inside the portal session you are already signed in to, the same way a person reading over your shoulder would.

  3. Teach it the form — once per form

    Nobody building this has ever seen the portal's form after login, so no field positions ship with the extension. Guessing would be worse than nothing: a wrong guess types a PAN into whatever box happened to match.

    So the first time, a CA teaches it:

    Open the real form→ "Capture this page"→ Match each box to a field→ Activate

    The capture records what boxes exist — their labels, types and options — and nothing that was typed in them. Back in the web app you say which of our fields belongs in each box: "Name of Applicant (as per PAN)" is client.legalName. Then you activate the map and the firm is done forever, until the portal changes.

    That matching is deliberately manual. It is a judgement, and the cost of getting it wrong is a misfiled government application.

Using it — every time

  1. Open the form, signed in as yourself

    The extension is disabled everywhere except the portal, and says so.

  2. Pick the case

    The list shows your firm's cases with their status and readiness. A case at 60% will fill 60% of the form, and tell you what it could not.

  3. Preview, or fill

    Preview reports what would happen and changes nothing on the page. Fill form actually types.

  4. Check every field

    Green outline: filled. Amber dashed: skipped, with a reason. Nothing: the extension never had anything to say about that box — including every CAPTCHA and password box, which it refuses by design.

  5. Solve the CAPTCHA and submit

    Yours. Always.

When the portal changes

Government portals get redesigned without notice. When the extension was taught the form it also recorded the form's shape — how many boxes, of what kinds, named what. Before filling anything it compares.

If the shape no longer matches

It stops and tells you the portal appears to have changed, and records that on the run. Capture the page again, re-check the matches, activate. The alternative — filling confidently into boxes that have moved — is the failure mode worth spending code to avoid.

Who can do what

Five roles. The differences are not about seniority — they are about which mistakes each person is in a position to make, and which of a client's secrets each person needs to see.

Role Can Cannot
Staff
data entry		 Add and edit clients and cases, fill in every section, run the checks, work the document checklist. See a full PAN or account number, record consent, or mark a case ready to file.
CA
the professional		 Everything Staff can, plus reveal identifiers (audited), record client consent, move a case between statuses, and use the browser extension. Invite users, change firm settings, or read the audit log.
Reviewer
read-only		 Read every case and the audit log. Useful for a partner or an internal check. Change anything at all.
Firm admin
runs the firm		 Everything above, plus invite and disable users, edit firm settings, and read the full audit log. Edit or delete an audit entry — nobody can. Or see another firm.
Platform admin
runs the service		 Create firms, set their licence, suspend and restore them, see counts. See any client, case, document or identifier — in any firm, including their own.
Why Staff cannot reveal a PAN

They do not need it. Entering a PAN and reading one back are different acts: typing it in comes from the document in front of them, reading it back out is how an identifier leaves the building. Every reveal, by anyone, writes a line in the audit log naming the person and the record.

Super admin & licences

A separate console, reached by a platform administrator, for running the service itself: which firms exist, what each one has paid for, and when that runs out.

The super admin cannot open a firm.

They see firm names, counts and licence state. They cannot see a client, a case, a document or an identifier — in any firm, including their own. Two separate things make that true. The account holds one permission, so every client, case, document and audit screen refuses it outright; and the queries it can run have no column for that data to arrive in, so even a mistake in the first layer returns nothing.

It is a different application

A super admin does not get the CA's app with half of it greyed out. They sign in to their own console — three screens, and no others exist for them:

Overview
What needs doing
Licences about to lapse, suspended firms, recently added firms, then the totals.
Firms
Every tenant
Status, licence, and usage against each limit. Add a firm, change a licence, suspend or restore.
Renewals
Who to call
Only what has ended or ends soon, expired first.

No Clients, no Cases, no browser extension, no Users, no firm settings, no audit log. Those screens would all be empty, and an empty screen reads as a fault rather than as a boundary.

Creating a firm

  1. Fill in the firm and its first administrator

    Name, short slug, optional FRN and contact details — and the name and email of the person who will run it. Both together, always: a firm nobody can sign in to is not a tenant, it is a row somebody has to clean up later.

  2. Set the licence

    Plan, the date it runs out, and the limits. A new firm defaults to a Trial that expires in 30 days.

  3. They receive an invitation, not a password

    The new administrator gets a single-use link, valid seven days, and chooses their own password. Nobody at the platform ever knows it — passwords are stored as a one-way hash and cannot be read back or reset into a known value.

What a licence controls

SettingEffect
Plan A label — Trial, Starter, Professional, Enterprise. Choosing one fills in the usual limits for that tier; you can then change any of them for this firm.
Valid until The date the licence expires. Leave it empty for a licence that does not expire.
Grace period Days after expiry during which nothing changes except a warning banner. Renewals are late; files should not stop because an invoice did.
Max users How many people the firm may have. Inviting past the limit is refused with a message naming the limit.
Max clients How many client businesses the firm may hold.
Max cases How many applications. Counted for the whole firm, not per year.

Any limit can be left empty, which means unlimited. The console shows each firm's usage against its limits — 18 / 25 clients — so a firm about to hit a wall is visible before they telephone about it.

What happens when a licence expires

Valid→ Expiring soon→ In grace→ Read-only
StateWhenWhat the firm sees
Valid Before expiry Nothing. Normal working.
Expiring soon Within 30 days A quiet banner with the date.
In grace After expiry, within the grace period A prominent banner. Everything still works.
Read-only After the grace period They can still sign in, read every case, and export. They cannot create or change anything until the licence is renewed.
Why read-only, and not locked out

These are a CA firm's professional records — statutory files they may be obliged to produce. Cutting off access to a client's case file because a renewal invoice is three weeks late would be an inappropriate thing for a piece of software to do. Expiry stops new work; it does not confiscate old work.

Suspension is the different lever. A suspended firm cannot sign in at all. That is for abuse or a demand from the firm itself, it requires a written reason, it is recorded in the audit trail, and it still deletes nothing.

Renewals

A second screen lists only the firms whose licence has ended or is about to, expired first and then soonest — because the useful question is never "show me every firm", it is "who do I have to telephone this week". Firms with no end date never appear there; there is nothing to renew.

Limits & safety

Some of these are promises about what the software will not do. They are worth reading, because several of them are the reason it is safe to point this thing at a government portal at all.

About the portal

It will neverWhy that holds
Press submitThere is no code anywhere in the extension that submits a form. A test reads the source and fails the build if one ever appears.
Touch a CAPTCHACAPTCHA boxes are refused by name, by label and by the box they sit in — checked when the form is learned, and checked again against the live page before anything is typed.
Read a password boxPassword, file and hidden boxes are refused outright. A refused box is recorded without any way to address it, so nothing downstream can reach it.
Store your portal loginIt never asks for one. It works inside the session you signed in to yourself, and it cannot read cookies.
Run on its ownNo timers, no background runs, no reacting to pages loading. Every single action starts with a click in the popup.
Run on any other siteIt is permitted on one address, and it checks the tab against the server's list before it will inject anything.
Send back what it typedThe record of a run holds which fields were attempted and what happened — filled, skipped, refused. There is nowhere in it for a value.

None of these are settings anybody can switch. They are properties of the code, asserted by tests that have each been deliberately broken once to confirm the test actually notices.

About eligibility

The system does not decide who is eligible.

21 structural checks run: formats, arithmetic, dates in the right order, required fields, documents, consent. A further 7 rules — the ones about turnover bands, subsidy percentages and qualifying periods — depend on the Government Resolution that actually constitutes the scheme, and that document has not been read. Those rules are off, their parameters are placeholders, and the screen lists exactly which checks are not running so nobody mistakes silence for approval.

Turning them on is a decision for someone holding the notification, not for someone who thinks the numbers look about right.

About your data

WhatHow it is kept
PAN, GSTIN, Udyam, loan accountEncrypted with a key belonging to your firm alone, tied to the exact row it sits in. Moved elsewhere, it will not decrypt — it does not quietly become someone else's identifier.
AadhaarLast four digits only. Storing a full Aadhaar is blocked by the database itself, pending a legal decision nobody has taken.
PasswordsA modern one-way hash. Cannot be read back, by anyone, including whoever runs the platform.
Invitation and reset linksSingle use, time limited, and stored only as a hash — the link in your email exists nowhere in the database.
DeletionsRecords are marked deleted, not erased. Retention defaults to eight years and the system only ever flags data that is ageing out — it never deletes on its own.
